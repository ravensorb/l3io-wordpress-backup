# Editorial Review — `addendum.md`

**Reviewed:** `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-py-wordpress-backup-2026-09-12/addendum.md` (1,701 words, 136 lines)
**Lenses run:** `structure`, `prose` (prose ran on top of the structure findings). Scope and strategy were explicitly out of scope and were not reviewed.
**Reader type:** humans · **Style guide:** Microsoft Writing Style Guide
**Content class:** docs

**Purpose/audience read:** This document exists to help the architecture, ADR and epic authors who pick up `prd.md` understand *how* each requirement could be built, what was considered and rejected, and what evidence stands behind both — so that downstream work does not re-litigate decisions the PRD deliberately left out of scope.

**Structure model applied:** Strategic/Context (Pyramid) — it is a decision-record companion to a PRD. The document mostly honors the model: each subsection leads with its status or conclusion and puts evidence below. Section C is where the model is currently breaking, because one entry in it has changed status and the surrounding scaffolding has not moved with it.

**Content is sacrosanct.** Nothing below challenges a decision, a fact, or a judgment. Every row is about placement, proportion, consistency of reference, or wording. Where a passage now disagrees with `prd.md`, the row reports the disagreement and leaves the resolution to the author.

## Verification of the C.2 rewrite against its surroundings

Three checks were run explicitly, because the request named them.

**1. Does any surviving passage still call `wpconfigr` maintained?** No. Every occurrence of "maintain" in the document was checked (lines 50, 58, 79). Line 58 is the corrected passage itself ("taking on unmaintained code"); line 50 applies "unmaintained" to `interconnect/it Search-Replace-DB`; line 79 applies "maintained" to `mysql-connector-python`. The `§A` table row for `cariad/py-wordpress-backup` says "Maintenance status unverified," which is a different package and does not conflict. **The factual correction is clean on its own terms.** The problems below are not residual overclaims — they are scaffolding and cross-references that did not move when the passage did.

**2. Do the addendum's section references into `prd.md` still resolve?** Yes, all five. `§1` (Vision), `§5.3` (Relocation), `§9` (Runtime Targets and Dependency Policy), `§11` (Operational Requirements — the Runbook bullet exists), `§13.1` (Non-Goals). Despite `§15`/`§16` being renumbered and rewritten, the addendum never cites either by number, so no `§` reference dangles.

**3. Do the FR references still resolve?** Yes, all of them. The addendum cites FR-2, 3, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 19, 20, 21, 22, 25, 26 and NFR-3, 4, 5, 6, 8. Every one of those is a defined heading in the current `prd.md`, and none exceeds FR-29. **No dangling FR numbers.** FR-27, FR-28 and FR-29 are never referenced — see row S12, which is a gap, not a dangling pointer.

**What did not survive the renumbering is the *question* references.** `prd.md §15` now opens "**None are open.**" and disposes of all eleven. The addendum still cites Q-6, Q-9 and Q-10 as live. That is where the contradictions are, and rows S2, S2b and S2c carry them.

## Summary

**Total recommendations:** 23 — 15 structure, 8 prose (one of which is an explicit PRESERVE).

**Estimated reduction if all cuts and condenses are accepted:** approximately 145–160 words removed, against a 1,701-word document — roughly **9%**. The reduction is concentrated almost entirely in §C.2, which would fall from 247 words to about 150, bringing it into proportion with C.1 (116). No length target was provided.

**Where the risk actually sits.** The rewrite itself is sound: the "maintained" overclaim is gone, no surviving passage reasserts it, and every `§` and FR reference in the document still resolves against the current `prd.md`. What did not keep up with the edit is the scaffolding around it:

1. **The section preamble now contradicts the section** (row 1). Cheapest fix in the list, highest cost if left.
2. **Three pointers cite questions `prd.md §15` has closed** — Q-6 twice and Q-10 once — and one of them (the version-range recommendation) is not merely stale but gives the opposite instruction to PRD Q-6. This is the only place in the review where the two documents would actively mislead someone acting on them.
3. **C.2 ¶2 now duplicates `prd.md §9` nearly fact-for-fact.** Worth naming plainly: the overclaim being corrected here survived in one document after being stated in two. Condensing the duplicate is the structural fix for the class of defect, not just this instance.

**Comprehension trade-offs.** Two recommendations trade detail for brevity and the author should weigh them rather than accept them by default. Condensing the duplicated evidence in C.2 ¶2 assumes the reader has `prd.md §9` to hand — defensible for a document whose first line is "Companion to `prd.md`," but it does make C.2 non-self-contained. And condensing the superseded-analysis paragraph removes the record that four options were once weighed; if that history is meant to stop a future reader from reopening the build-or-buy question, keep a one-clause trace of it rather than cutting to nothing.

**Not reviewed, by instruction:** scope and strategy. No finding above turns on whether a decision is correct — only on where it sits, how it is labeled, and whether the document agrees with itself and with `prd.md`.
